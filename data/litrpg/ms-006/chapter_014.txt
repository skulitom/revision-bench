The air was thick with the scent of brine and decay as Sorrel stepped cautiously onto the Drowned Steps. The ancient stone stairs, now half-submerged in murky water, led to an unknown destination below. Each step groaned underfoot, a symphony of creaks and splashes that echoed through the cavernous space.

Beside Sorrel, Bex shifted nervously, her eyes darting around as if expecting something—or someone—to leap out from the shadows. "You sure this is the way?" she asked, her voice barely above a whisper.

Sorrel nodded, feeling the familiar weight of the grey cloak draped over her shoulders. It was a comforting presence, a silent guardian against the chill and the unknown. "This is the only path," Sorrel reassured, though her own heart raced with anticipation and a hint of fear.

As they descended further, the light from above dimmed, replaced by an eerie luminescence that seemed to emanate from the water itself. Strange, glowing algae painted the steps in shades of green and blue, casting an otherworldly glow on Sorrel's face.

Suddenly, a sharp pain lanced through her foot. Sorrel stumbled, nearly losing her balance, as a hidden spike jutted out from the steps. She cursed under her breath, pulling her foot back just in time to avoid further injury. "Watch your step," she warned Bex, who was now several steps behind.

Bex nodded, her eyes wide with apprehension. "I'll try," she replied, though her voice wavered slightly.

Sorrel pressed on, her mind already racing through possible strategies. The Drowned Steps were treacherous, but they had to keep moving. As they reached the bottom, Sorrel's hand brushed against something cold and metallic in her pocket. She froze, realizing with a sinking feeling that her copper ring was gone.

"Damn it," she muttered, her frustration bubbling over. "We can't afford to lose anything now."

Bex turned, concern etched across her face. "What happened?"

Sorrel shook her head, trying to shake off the irritation. "I don't know. Must've slipped out somewhere along the way."

She took a deep breath, steeling herself for what lay ahead. The Drowned Steps had tested them already, and they were only just beginning.
